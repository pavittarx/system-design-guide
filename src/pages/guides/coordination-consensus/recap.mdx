<Recap>

**You cannot tell slow from dead.** Every failure detector is a timeout, and every timeout is a guess with a cost on both sides.

**FLP says perfect consensus is impossible; Raft chooses safety over liveness.** It will never decide two things; it may occasionally decide nothing for a moment.

**Elect a leader.** One writer removes whole categories of conflict. Pay for it with a failover window on every leader death.

**Raft = terms + elections + a replicated log.** Commit means a majority has it; an election winner must have every committed entry; a newer term makes an old leader step down.

**Odd numbers only.** 3 tolerates 1, 5 tolerates 2, 4 tolerates the same as 3 while being slower. Bigger clusters are slower, not faster.

**Fencing tokens are non-negotiable** for any lock protecting correctness. The resource rejects stale tokens; the client cannot be trusted to notice it was paused.

**Leases are locks with expiry** — timed by the store, on a monotonic clock, never by the client.

**Classify your locks.** Efficiency locks can be cheap and occasionally wrong. Correctness locks need consensus plus fencing, or they are decoration.

**Coordination stores hold decisions, not data.** Low thousands of writes/second, tens of megabytes. Watch and cache; never put them on the request path.

**The best coordination is none.** Partition ownership, make operations idempotent, use database constraints, prefer commutative operations.

</Recap>
