<Recap>

**You can't tell slow from dead.** A missing reply looks the same either way, so every failure detector is a timeout, and every timeout is a guess that can be wrong in both directions.

**No algorithm can guarantee agreement under every timing (FLP); Raft picks safety.** It will never decide two conflicting things, but it may occasionally decide nothing for a moment.

**Pick one leader.** One writer removes whole kinds of conflict. The price is a gap in service every time the leader dies.

**Raft is numbered terms, majority-vote elections and a shared log.** A write is committed once a majority has it; only a node with every committed write can win an election; a leader that sees a newer term steps down.

**Use odd numbers.** 3 nodes survive 1 failure, 5 survive 2, and 4 survive no more than 3 while being slower. Bigger clusters are slower, not faster.

**Fencing tokens are non-negotiable** for any lock that protects correctness. The storage rejects writes with an old token, because a paused client can't tell it was paused.

**A lease is a lock that expires.** Time it at the store, on a clock that only counts forward, never on the client.

**Decide what your lock protects.** Efficiency locks can be cheap and occasionally wrong. Correctness locks need consensus plus fencing, or they're decoration.

**Coordination stores hold decisions, not data.** One throughput limit for the whole cluster, data in the tens of megabytes. Watch and cache; never call them on every request.

**The best coordination is none.** Give each key one owner, make operations safe to repeat, let the database enforce rules, and prefer operations whose order doesn't matter.

</Recap>
