<Recap>

- **Monitoring answers known questions; observability answers new ones.** The failure that takes you down is the one nobody put on the dashboard.

- **Three signals, three jobs.** Metrics say *that* something is wrong (cheap, but cardinality-bound). Traces say *where* (sampled). Logs say *why* (linear in traffic, therefore expensive).

- **Averages hide everything that matters.** 99 requests at 20 ms and one at 2 s averages to a healthy-looking 40 ms. Use percentiles — and never average percentiles across hosts; aggregate buckets instead.

- **Watch saturation, not just utilization.** Utilization stops carrying information at 100%; queue depth keeps counting.

- **Cardinality is a multiplication.** Labels must be bounded and small. Never a user ID, a raw URL path, or anything from user input. Use events and exemplars for the unbounded stuff.

- **An SLO turns reliability into a budget.** 99.9% over 30 days is 43.2 minutes. Spend the budget deliberately; agree the freeze policy before you need it.

- **Alert on symptoms and burn rate, never on causes.** Add a minimum-volume floor so 3 a.m. arithmetic doesn't page anyone.

- **Missing data is not zero,** and a monitoring system that fails silently is worse than none.

</Recap>
