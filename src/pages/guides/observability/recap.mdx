<Recap>

- **Monitoring answers the questions you wrote down; observability answers new ones.** The failure that takes you down is the one nobody put on the dashboard.

- **Three signals, three jobs.** Metrics say *that* something is wrong (cheap per request, expensive per label). Traces say *where* (sampled). Logs say *why* (cost grows with every request).

- **Averages hide the people who suffered.** 99 requests at 20 ms and one at 2 s average to a healthy-looking 40 ms. Use percentiles, and never average them across hosts: add the histogram buckets, then compute once.

- **Watch the queue, not just how busy something is.** Busy-ness stops at 100%; the number of things waiting keeps counting.

- **Every label combination is a separate series.** Keep labels to a small, fixed set of values. Never a user ID, a raw URL path or anything typed by a user; put those in events, or link to them with exemplars.

- **An SLO turns "reliable enough" into a budget.** 99.9% over 30 days is 43.2 minutes of failure. Spend it deliberately, and agree what happens when it runs out before it does.

- **Page on what users feel, and on how fast the budget is burning, not on possible causes.** Add a minimum-volume floor so two requests at 3 a.m. can't page anyone.

- **No data is not the same as zero,** and a monitoring system that fails silently is worse than none.

</Recap>

</Collapse>
