<Recap>

- **The API outlives the implementation.** Design the contract so a full backend rewrite wouldn't change it.

- **REST buys the HTTP ecosystem; RPC buys efficiency; GraphQL buys one round trip** — and each hands you a different bill. GraphQL's is unbounded query cost, so depth limits, cost analysis and batching are mandatory.

- **Additive changes are safe; removals, new enum values, tightened validation and changed defaults are not.** The last two break silently.

- **Cursor pagination over offset,** always with a unique tiebreaker in the sort, or you will duplicate and skip rows.

- **4xx means don't retry; 5xx means you might.** Never 200 with an error body — it poisons caches, metrics and client error handling at once.

- **Gateways do cross-cutting concerns only.** Authentication at the edge, authorization in the service that owns the resource. Business logic in the gateway becomes an ESB.

- **Token bucket or sliding-window counter** for almost everything; fixed windows let clients get 2× the limit across a boundary.

- **Distributed limits are a coordination problem.** Divide-the-limit is cheap and wrong; a shared counter is accurate and adds a round trip plus a dependency. Decide fail-open vs fail-closed before it fails.

- **Return 429 with Retry-After — and make sure clients jitter it,** or you've synchronised your traffic instead of spreading it.

</Recap>
