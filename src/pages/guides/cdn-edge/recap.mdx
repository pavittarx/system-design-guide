<Recap>

**Distance has a hard floor.** Light in fibre travels about 200,000 km/s, so London to New York costs about 60 ms per round trip, and opening an HTTPS connection and getting the first byte takes three (four on TLS 1.2). No code change gets that back. Only being closer does.

**A PoP is a nearby cache that also handles your handshakes.** On a hit it saves the whole trip. On everything else it still saves the handshake round trips, which is why a CDN speeds up pages it can't cache.

**Two ways to reach the nearest PoP.** Anycast announces one address from everywhere and lets internet routing pick. DNS steering hands out different addresses, but it sees the user's resolver, not the user, unless EDNS Client Subnet passes the user's subnet along.

**A short TTL caps origin load.** Each PoP fetches an object at most 60 ÷ TTL times a minute, however much traffic arrives. Short TTLs only hurt when users wait for the refresh.

**The cache key decides your hit rate.** Everything you add to it splits the cache. `Vary: Cookie` means one copy per user, which is no cache at all.

**Shield your origin.** Without one, 100 PoPs missing together send 100 identical fetches to your origin. With one, it's 1.

**Change the file name instead of purging.** A content hash in the filename makes every deploy a new key. When you must purge, prefer soft purges and purging by tag.

**Serve old copies on purpose.** `stale-while-revalidate` means no user waits for a refresh; `stale-if-error` turns an origin outage into slightly old pages.

**Edge code is a filter, not a backend.** If it needs your database, run it next to your database.

**CDNs fail through configuration.** Both famous outages were a config change pushed everywhere in seconds. Keep your origin able to survive without the CDN, and your DNS TTLs short.

</Recap>
